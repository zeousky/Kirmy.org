bicycles are suspiciously efficient

two wheels, some bearings, and an unreasonable amount of transportation

A bicycle is one of those machines that seems almost too simple for how useful it is. Human legs turn cranks, a chain moves the rear wheel, and suddenly one person can travel several times faster than walking using remarkably little energy.

why two wheels work

A moving bicycle balances through a combination of steering geometry, rider input, wheel dynamics, and continuous small corrections.

The old explanation that gyroscopic effects alone keep bikes upright is incomplete. Steering and geometry matter enormously.

gears make humans less terrible motors

Bicycle gears trade force for speed. Low gears make climbing easier by letting the rider turn the pedals more times for each wheel rotation.

High gears move the bike farther per pedal revolution, which is useful on flat roads or descents.

efficiency

Bicycles have low rolling resistance, relatively little mechanical loss, and no need to carry a heavy engine or fuel supply.

On suitable terrain, cycling can move a person using less energy per kilometer than almost any other common form of personal transport.

why cities care

Bicycles take little space compared with cars, produce no direct exhaust, and work well for short urban trips.

Their usefulness depends heavily on safe infrastructure. Paint beside fast traffic is technically infrastructure in the same way a napkin is technically armor.

small facts
  • Modern bicycles commonly use pneumatic tires.
  • Derailleurs move the chain between different sprockets.
  • Disc brakes are now common on many bike types.
  • Cargo bikes can replace cars for some local trips.
