electromagnetic waves, antennas, broadcasting, and voices crossing empty space

Radio works because changing electric and magnetic fields can travel through space as waves. Encode information into those waves, transmit them from an antenna, and another antenna can recover the signal somewhere else. This remains ridiculous in the best possible way.

what is actually traveling

Radio waves are part of the electromagnetic spectrum, the same broad family that includes visible light, infrared, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.

They differ mainly in frequency and wavelength, which affects how they propagate and interact with matter.

putting sound onto a carrier

A transmitter can encode audio by varying characteristics of a higher-frequency carrier wave.

AM varies amplitude. FM varies frequency. Digital radio uses more complex methods to encode bits into transmitted signals.

antennas

An antenna converts electrical signals into electromagnetic radiation and does the reverse on reception.

Its shape and size matter because antennas interact most efficiently with certain wavelengths and polarizations.

why radio goes far

Some frequencies travel mostly in straight lines, while others can bend around obstacles, reflect from layers of the atmosphere, or follow the ground.

That is why different radio bands are suited to broadcasting, mobile phones, satellites, aviation, radar, and countless other systems.

small facts
  • Radio does not require air to travel.
  • FM radio is generally more resistant to amplitude noise than AM.
  • Antennas can transmit and receive using the same physical principles.
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth also use radio waves.
