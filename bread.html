bread is controlled microbial inflation

flour, water, yeast, heat, and several thousand years of humans being hungry

Bread is mostly flour and water made vastly more interesting by fermentation and heat. It is one of humanity's oldest staple foods, which means we have spent millennia getting emotionally attached to different ways of making wet grain become delicious.

gluten builds the structure

When wheat flour is mixed with water, proteins including glutenin and gliadin interact to form gluten.

That elastic network traps gas and lets dough expand instead of simply leaking bubbles into the room.

yeast makes the bubbles

Baker's yeast consumes sugars and produces carbon dioxide and ethanol. The carbon dioxide gets trapped inside the dough and creates the crumb structure.

Sourdough adds a community of yeasts and lactic acid bacteria, producing additional acids and flavor compounds.

fermentation is flavor

Longer fermentation gives enzymes and microbes more time to transform starches, sugars, and proteins.

This can produce deeper flavor, improve browning, and change texture. It also explains why two breads made from nearly identical ingredients can taste completely different.

the oven finishes the crime

Heat rapidly expands gases, sets starches, firms proteins, drives off water, and browns the crust.

The Maillard reaction and caramelization contribute many of the aromas and flavors associated with baked bread.

small facts
  • Salt strengthens dough and slows fermentation.
  • Steam helps some breads develop a thin, crisp crust.
  • Whole-grain flour includes more of the grain kernel than white flour.
  • Different wheat varieties produce flours with different protein levels.
