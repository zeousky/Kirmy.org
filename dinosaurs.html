dinosaurs never really left

feathers, fossils, mass extinction, and the birds currently screaming outside

Dinosaurs dominated terrestrial ecosystems for more than 150 million years. Then a mass extinction wiped out every non-avian dinosaur lineage. One branch survived, however, meaning modern birds are not merely descended from dinosaurs. They are dinosaurs.

what counts as a dinosaur

Dinosaurs are a specific evolutionary group of archosaurs. Not every prehistoric reptile belongs to it.

Pterosaurs were flying relatives, not dinosaurs. Marine reptiles such as plesiosaurs and mosasaurs were also separate groups. Museums have been trying to explain this for decades while gift shops quietly sell everything together.

feathers changed the picture

Fossils from many dinosaur species preserve feathers or feather-like structures.

This evidence helped establish that feathers evolved before modern flight and likely served roles such as insulation, display, camouflage, and brooding.

the asteroid

About 66 million years ago, a large asteroid struck near what is now the Yucatán Peninsula.

The impact triggered global environmental disruption including fires, atmospheric dust, reduced sunlight, cooling, and food-web collapse.

birds survived

Some small feathered dinosaur lineages survived the extinction event and diversified into modern birds.

So the next time a pigeon steals food from a station floor, show some respect. Its family survived an asteroid and now it has your sandwich.

small facts
  • Birds are living theropod dinosaurs.
  • Tyrannosaurus rex lived closer in time to humans than to Stegosaurus.
  • Many dinosaurs likely had complex social and reproductive behaviors.
  • Fossilized footprints can reveal speed, gait, and group movement.
