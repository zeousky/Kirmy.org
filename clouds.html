clouds are less fluffy than advertised

tiny droplets, ice crystals, weather, and deceptive softness

A cloud is a visible collection of tiny water droplets, ice crystals, or both suspended in the atmosphere. From below, it looks soft enough to nap on. Inside, it is damp air, turbulence, and physics having a group project.

how a cloud forms

Air contains invisible water vapor. When moist air cools enough, some of that vapor condenses onto microscopic particles such as dust, salt, or smoke.

Billions of tiny droplets together scatter enough light to become visible as a cloud.

why clouds float

Individual cloud droplets are extremely small and fall very slowly. Rising air currents and turbulence can easily keep them suspended.

The cloud as a whole is not weightless. Large clouds can contain enormous amounts of water spread across a huge volume.

cloud families

Meteorologists classify clouds by height, shape, and development. Cirrus clouds are thin and high, stratus clouds form sheets, and cumulus clouds grow in rounded heaps.

Cumulonimbus clouds are the dramatic towers associated with thunderstorms. They are basically cumulus clouds after choosing violence.

rain begins inside

For rain to fall, tiny droplets or ice particles need to grow large enough that gravity wins over the air currents holding them up.

Different processes dominate in warm and cold clouds, but collisions, coalescence, freezing, and melting all play roles.

small facts
  • Fog is essentially a cloud at ground level.
  • Cloud droplets are much smaller than ordinary raindrops.
  • High clouds often consist mostly of ice crystals.
  • Clouds strongly influence both weather and Earth's energy balance.
