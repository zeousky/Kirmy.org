Why God Isn't Real

A direct, evidence-based case
The biggest claim in human history deserves the highest standard of scrutiny.

For thousands of years, humans have believed in gods. Today, roughly half the world’s population still does. The question is not whether belief feels comforting or culturally important. The question is whether any god actually exists.

Core claim examined

The God of classical monotheism is usually described as:

  • Omnipotent - all-powerful
  • Omniscient - all-knowing
  • Omnibenevolent - perfectly good and loving
  • Creator of the universe who cares about human lives and wants a relationship

If such a being existed, we would expect clear, unambiguous evidence. We find the opposite.

1. The concept is logically incoherent

Before we even look at evidence, the standard definition of God contains fatal contradictions.

The omnipotence paradox

Can an all-powerful God create a stone so heavy that even He cannot lift it?

  • If yes - then He is not all-powerful (He cannot lift the stone).
  • If no - then He is not all-powerful (He cannot create such a stone).

This is not wordplay. It reveals that “all-powerful” is not a coherent property. Limits are built into the idea of power itself.

Omniscience and free will

If God knows the future perfectly, including every choice you will ever make, then your choices are not free in any meaningful sense - they are already fixed. If your choices are genuinely free, then God cannot know them in advance.

Either God is not omniscient, or humans do not have libertarian free will. Both cannot be true simultaneously. - Standard philosophical formulation

Omnibenevolence + power + knowledge

These three attributes together create the famous trilemma. We will explore it fully in the next section.

2. The problem of evil

This is the oldest and strongest argument against an all-good, all-powerful God.

Is God willing to prevent evil, but not able?
Then he is not omnipotent.
Is he able, but not willing?
Then he is malevolent.
Is he both able and willing?
Then whence cometh evil?
Is he neither able nor willing?
Then why call him God? - Epicurus (341-270 BCE)

The world contains staggering amounts of suffering that cannot be justified by any coherent “greater good”:

  • Children dying of bone cancer before they can speak
  • Millions of animals eaten alive by predators every day for hundreds of millions of years before humans existed
  • Earthquakes, tsunamis, and diseases that kill indiscriminately
  • Genocides, tortures, and deliberate cruelty across history

The usual replies fail:

  • “Free will excuses it” - Does not explain natural disasters, childhood disease, or animal suffering that predates humans.
  • “We can’t understand God’s plan” - This is special pleading. If we cannot judge goodness, then the claim that God is good becomes meaningless.
  • “Suffering builds character” - Not for the millions who die in agony as infants. Character-building does not require leukemia.
Even one instance of apparently pointless suffering is enough to break the claim of perfect benevolence + unlimited power. There are billions.

3. Divine hiddenness

If a perfectly loving God exists and wants a personal relationship with every human, we should expect overwhelming, accessible evidence of His existence.

Instead we have:

  • Complete silence from the sky
  • No unambiguous, public miracles in the modern era
  • Billions of sincere, intelligent people who have prayed, studied, and begged for belief - and received nothing
  • Geographic and cultural distribution of belief that perfectly tracks human history and migration, not divine revelation

A God who hides so effectively that most people either believe the wrong version or believe nothing at all is indistinguishable from a God who does not exist.

4. Complete absence of reliable evidence

Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.

The claim “an invisible, immaterial mind created the universe and cares who you have sex with” is the most extraordinary claim possible. The evidence offered is remarkably weak:

Personal experience

People report feelings of presence, answered prayers, and “signs.” These are indistinguishable from the experiences reported by followers of every other religion and from well-understood psychological phenomena (confirmation bias, emotional salience, sleep paralysis, temporal lobe activity, etc.).

Miracles and prayer

Double-blind studies on intercessory prayer show no effect beyond chance. Claims of modern miracles collapse under investigation. Whenever a claimed miracle occurs in a way that can be properly recorded and scrutinized, it disappears.

Holy books

These are claims, not evidence. Every religion has them. They contradict each other and often themselves.

After thousands of years, not a single piece of evidence has met scientific standards of reproducibility and falsifiability.

5. Science explains everything we once attributed to gods

Cosmology

We understand the early universe back to fractions of a second after the Big Bang. We do not need a creator to explain its beginning. Quantum fields, inflationary models, and various cyclic or multiverse proposals show that “something from nothing” is not obviously impossible or even required. “God did it” explains nothing and predicts nothing.

Biology and evolution

Darwin’s dangerous idea works. Natural selection acting on variation produces complex, apparently designed structures over deep time. The fossil record, genetics, embryology, and observed speciation all confirm this. No designer is required. The “god of the gaps” keeps shrinking.

Neuroscience and consciousness

Every aspect of religious experience - awe, mystical states, hearing voices, feeling presence - can be induced by brain stimulation, drugs, oxygen deprivation, epilepsy, and meditation. There is no evidence of a soul separate from brain activity. When the brain dies, the person is gone.

Morality

Cooperative, altruistic, and “moral” behavior exists in many social species. Human morality is an evolved feature of group-living primates plus cultural evolution. We do not need divine command theory to explain why we generally dislike needless suffering.

6. Religious texts undermine themselves

Even if we ignore science, the primary texts used to justify belief contain fatal problems.

  • Internal contradictions (how Judas died, how many women visited the tomb, the order of creation in Genesis 1 vs 2, etc.)
  • Historical inaccuracies (no evidence for a global flood, no evidence for the Exodus as described, census under Quirinius does not match the nativity story)
  • Moral horrors presented as divine commands (slavery regulations, genocide of Canaanites, death for apostasy, stoning for adultery, etc.)
  • Clear signs of human authorship: scientific errors (firmament, talking donkeys treated as normal, value of pi), copied stories from earlier cultures, and progressive theological development over time

The texts read exactly like what they are: products of Iron Age and late antique human cultures trying to make sense of their world.

7. We know exactly why humans invent gods

Religious belief is not mysterious. Multiple converging fields explain its persistence:

  • Hyperactive Agency Detection (HADD) - Brains that assume agents are present when they hear rustling survive better than brains that assume wind. False positives produce gods.
  • Theory of mind over-application - We are extremely good at inferring minds. We apply the same machinery to the weather, the universe, and misfortune.
  • Death anxiety and meaning - Belief in an afterlife and cosmic purpose reduces terror. This is a feature, not evidence.
  • Social cohesion and control - Gods are extremely useful for enforcing in-group norms, justifying hierarchy, and motivating large-scale cooperation (and warfare).
  • Cultural evolution - Memes that are memorable, emotionally activating, and promise rewards/punishments spread. This has nothing to do with truth.

Every culture has invented gods that conveniently match its environment, politics, and moral intuitions. Yahweh looked like an ancient Near Eastern war god because that is exactly what he was before later theological upgrades.

9. "God is all-powerful!" So why doesn't he just make the world perfect?

Christians are always saying "God is all powerful! He can do anything! Nothing is impossible for him!" Alright then. If that's true, explain why the world is still a complete disaster.

If God is truly all-powerful, all-knowing, and all-loving, then he should be able to just snap his fingers and make everything perfect. So why doesn't he?

  • Why doesn't he heal every single sick person, every cancer patient, every disabled person right now?
  • Why doesn't he just end all wars immediately? No more bombs, no more soldiers dying, no more civilians getting slaughtered.
  • Why are there still children starving to death every day while he supposedly loves everyone?
  • Why doesn't he remove every sickness, every virus, every disease from the entire planet?
  • Why let natural disasters happen - earthquakes, hurricanes, floods, tsunamis that kill thousands of innocent people?
  • Why not just kill all the bad people? All the rapists, child molesters, murderers, abusers - just make them disappear or drop dead. No trial needed if you're all powerful.
  • Why not make the whole world a perfect paradise? No poverty, no hunger, no pain, no suffering, no death. Just... perfect.
  • Why let homophobia and transphobia keep destroying lives when he could snap it out of existence?
  • Why create people he knows in advance will suffer horribly or end up in hell?
  • Why not just forgive everyone and make heaven on Earth instead of playing these weird games with free will and tests?

This is the big one. If God can do literally anything, why is the world still so fucked up? Why doesn't he just fix it all?

The excuses are garbage

  • "Free will" - Okay, but that doesn't explain cancer in babies, birth defects, or earthquakes. Those aren't about free will.
  • "It's part of his mysterious plan" - So his plan includes kids getting raped and entire villages getting wiped out by floods? That's a fucked up plan.
  • "We can't understand God's ways" - Then stop claiming he's all good and all loving. If we can't judge him, you can't either.
  • "Suffering makes us stronger" - Tell that to the millions of people who die in agony. Dead people don't get stronger.

Here's the simple logic: If God can make the world perfect but chooses not to, he's evil. If he wants to make the world perfect but can't, then he's not all-powerful. Either way, the "all powerful loving God" story falls apart.

Christians keep telling us God is out there watching over everyone. Cool. Then why is he just watching while the world burns?

10. Your religion is almost certainly just an accident of birth

Here's one of the biggest tells that religion is made up by humans:

If you were born in Pakistan, the chances are overwhelming you'd be a devout Muslim who thinks the Bible is corrupted and Jesus was just a prophet.

If you were born in rural India, you'd probably believe in multiple gods and reincarnation.

If you grew up in a strict Catholic family in the Philippines, you'd be Catholic.

If you were raised by atheist parents in Scandinavia, you'd probably be an atheist.

The "one true religion" you believe in is overwhelmingly determined by where you were born and who raised you. Not by evidence. Not by a personal encounter with the divine. Just random chance of geography and family.

A God who actually wanted a real relationship with every human being would not set up a system where the vast majority of people are born into the "wrong" religion through no fault of their own. That would be an incredibly cruel and stupid design.

Especially fucked up for queer people: millions are born into families and cultures that teach them their love is an abomination and they'll burn for it. A loving God wouldn't make whether someone ends up hated by their own parents depend on the zip code they were born in.

Instead, this is exactly what you'd expect if religions are just human inventions that spread the same way languages, clothing styles, and food preferences do - through culture and upbringing.

Why would an all-powerful, all-knowing, perfect being need any of that?

  • Why does he care so much if tiny humans on one tiny planet praise him or not?
  • Why get angry about "graven images" or other gods existing?
  • Why set up a system where you have to believe in him or suffer forever, like some toxic partner who says "love me or else"?

This doesn't sound like a transcendent, perfect deity. This sounds exactly like an insecure ancient king, or a jealous boyfriend, or a cult leader who needs constant validation and control.

A truly great being wouldn't be so obsessed with being worshipped. A truly powerful being wouldn't throw fits when people don't believe the right things.

The God of the Bible has the emotional maturity and ego issues of a flawed human. Almost like... he was invented by flawed humans.

Real power doesn't need constant "you're so great" from its creations. Real love doesn't threaten infinite punishment for doubt.

15. The burden of proof

The person making the positive existential claim carries the burden. “There is an invisible, all-powerful being” is a claim. “I do not believe you” is the default response until good evidence arrives.

You do not need to disprove every possible god, any more than you need to disprove Russell’s teapot orbiting the sun. The more specific and powerful the god claim, the easier it is to falsify - and the classical omni-God has been falsified repeatedly.

Agnostic atheism is the accurate position: we cannot be 100% certain about unfalsifiable claims, but we have no good reason to believe and overwhelming reason to reject the specific god claims presented to us.

16. Common objections answered

“But what if God is testing our faith?”
An all-knowing God would already know who would pass or fail. A test that includes the eternal torture of people who were never given sufficient evidence is not compatible with perfect goodness.
“Science can’t explain everything.”
True. But “I don’t know” is not evidence for “God did it.” God is not a simpler or better explanation; it is an additional entity that itself requires explanation.
“Without God, life has no meaning.”
Meaning is something conscious beings create. The absence of a cosmic dictator is not terrifying - it is liberating. Your relationships, projects, art, love, and curiosity are no less real because they are not commanded from above.
“I feel God / He changed my life.”
Subjective experience is real to the person having it. It is not evidence that the cause of the experience is an external deity rather than the brain’s own powerful capacity for meaning-making, community, and emotional transformation.
“But the universe is so finely tuned!”
We do not know if the constants could have been different. Even if they could, a multiverse or unknown selection effect is a far simpler hypothesis than an intelligent agent who just happens to have exactly the mind needed to fine-tune for life and then remains undetectable.
